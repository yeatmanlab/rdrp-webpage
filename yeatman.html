Teaching & Mentorship Philosophy

"Great science starts with curiosity…. That drives the creativity, and the perseverance, and hopefully the rigor as well…. I think science is beautiful. What is more beautiful than discovering something that has never been known before?"
— Ben Barres, "On Beauty and Curiosity in Science," 2017

Inspiring curiosity and an appreciation for the beauty of science is the overarching goal of Jason's teaching and mentorship, in three ways:

Enthusiasm

Letting his own excitement for the material bolster his lectures, and trying to inspire in every student and postdoc a curiosity and love for the scientific method.

Hands-On Experience

Every course gives students the chance to design experiments, work with real data, and — in several courses — examine mechanisms of learning in their own brain at the MRI center.

Team Science

Inspiring students and postdocs to work together, learn from each other, and treat their research as a collaborative journey rather than a solo pursuit.

His course Measuring Learning in the Brain embodies this philosophy. In Spring 2021, after learning students were hungry for in-person experience, he moved the class outdoors — small groups worked on experiment design under an oak tree on campus, then ran their own studies as final projects at the Stanford MRI center, designing experiments to probe mechanisms of learning in their own brains. Many students described it as transformative.

Recognized with 5/5 course evaluations across his Stanford courses, and the Stanford Department of Pediatrics Postdoc Mentor Award (2021).
