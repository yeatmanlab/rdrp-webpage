One lab. Two lenses on how kids learn to read.

The Reading & Dyslexia Research Program and the Brain Development & Education Lab are two sides of the same coin — both directed by Prof. Jason D. Yeatman, both asking how children learn to read and why some struggle. Use the toggle above to switch lenses, or explore ROAR, the assessment platform that grew out of both.

Current Research

Reading disabilities cannot be distilled to a single, simple cause. We take an interdisciplinary approach — spanning education, pediatrics, psychology, neuroscience, and engineering — studying readers of all kinds, at every age.

Research Focus

We study the interplay between brain maturation and cognitive development — how the brain's reading circuitry develops in response to education, and how targeted interventions change the brains of children with dyslexia.

01

Eye Spy

Investigating visual attention in reading — how readers deploy attention across letters and words.

02

Brain Pictures

Brain Pictures with Stanford RDRP — neuroimaging studies mapping the reading brain across development.

03

ROAR

Our flagship translational tool — a validated, automated reading assessment used in classrooms nationwide.

01

Reading Circuit Development

How the brain's reading circuitry develops in response to education, from early childhood onward.

02

Neuroplasticity & Intervention

How targeted behavioral interventions prompt measurable changes in the brains of children with dyslexia.

03

Longitudinal Brain Imaging

Tracking brain maturation and cognitive development over time with MRI, in collaboration with the Wu Tsai Neuroscience Institute.

Built on our research

ROAR — Rapid Online Assessment of Reading

An open-access, fully automated reading assessment platform grounded in our ongoing research. No test administrator required — just precise, real-time indices of reading ability, with instructional score reports for teachers.
